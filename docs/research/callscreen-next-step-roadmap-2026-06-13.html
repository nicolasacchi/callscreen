Callscreen — Next-Step Roadmap (June 2026)

A healthy, deployed product (nine of ten review dimensions "solid", 472 tests green): the next chapter is to close the remaining call-flow reliability holes, land the deferred ops/supply-chain hardening, and make the product smarter and actually Italian.

2026-06-13 · status:applied · project:callscreen · 80 verified findings

Executive assessment

Callscreen is a genuinely healthy, deployed product. The May-2026 multi-view review's entire P0–P3 roadmap landed and held — verified in the current code: atomic flow_state claims, durable throttles, CSP, a DB-aware healthcheck, runtime FKs, and 461 (now 472) green tests. The core screen → Whisper → Kimi → ntfy flow is well-tested and operationally honest.

This review is therefore not a debt cleanup but a forward step. It surfaces three things worth acting on: a small cluster of real reliability gaps at the call-flow edges (a failed transfer strands the caller; a raising goodbye could drop a real caller with zero notification; aggregate alerting is absent); the still-open deferred items (Python supply-chain pinning, the unhealthchecked Whisper sidecar, DB CHECK/FK constraints); and the product's single biggest ceiling — it never learns from operator feedback and is 100% English despite being explicitly Italian-primary.

The next chapter is two-pronged: (1) close the call-flow reliability holes and land the deferred ops/supply-chain hardening so the deployed system is bulletproof; then (2) make the product smarter and reach its actual audience — a feedback-driven classifier loop, voicemail TL;DR summaries, and Italian i18n. The highest-leverage architecture investment, extracting the call FSM and the audio/voice layer out of the 676-line TelnyxController, is the enabling refactor that makes both safer and testable.

Strategic bets — the product's next chapter

Five bets, larger than the tier items, that most move the product forward. The first two are the true differentiators; the third is the enabler.

SB-1 · Feedback-driven classifier learning loop m

The single highest-leverage product gap: the system never learns. Operator whitelist/spam/legit feedback only flips Contact flags and writes audit rows; nothing reaches the classifier. Mark the same telemarketer spam ten times and call eleven is classified from scratch. Worse, manual mark_spam doesn't even feed the auto-blacklist heuristic — the highest-signal case (LLM said legit, operator overrode) contributes nothing. Closing this loop turns every correction into durable intelligence.

SB-2 · Real i18n — make the Italian-primary product actually Italian xl

The clearest mismatch between positioning and reality: an explicitly Italian-primary product ships a 100% hardcoded-English admin console (en.yml is still the Rails scaffold; no it.yml, no default_locale) plus unstyled English Devise auth screens. The Italian operator is the end-user. Highest UX leverage for the stated audience.

SB-3 · Extract the call FSM + audio/voice/auth layers l

TelnyxController is a 676-line god-object whose FSM is an unguarded string column with transitions scattered across three files and three hand-maintained subsets that can silently diverge. This is the enabling refactor — it makes the reliability fixes and FSM guards directly unit-testable and shrinks future-bug surface. The audio/voice/auth extractions are independent and can land first to lower risk.

SB-4 · Land the deferred ops & supply-chain hardening m

The operational soft underbelly of an otherwise-bulletproof deploy: an unhealthchecked Whisper sidecar on :latest, an entirely unpinned Python TTS chain (non-reproducible builds), and no aggregate signal when sweeps fire repeatedly or the dead set grows. A systemic problem currently produces only log lines no one watches. (Combines P1-6 + P2-10 + P1-8.)

SB-5 · Graceful Whisper-outage degradation + STIR/SHAKEN attestation m

Two compounding resilience steps. A Whisper outage today marks a real caller :failed with no listen link though the recording exists — should degrade to a plain voicemail. Separately, Telnyx delivers STIR/SHAKEN attestation free in the webhook; consuming it gives a cheap anti-spoofing signal aligned with this product's exact threat model (Italian mobile-prefix spoofing) — as a soft input, never a hard block.

Method & provenance

Ten specialised reviewers fanned out across the codebase (security, reliability, architecture, performance, data model, tests, code quality, ops/supply-chain, UI/UX/i18n/a11y, product). Each finding was adversarially verified against the actual code by an independent skeptic — false-positives and already-fixed items were dropped — yielding 80 retained findings, then synthesised into this roadmap. Effort scale: xs < s < m < l < xl. This builds on, and does not re-report, the May 2026 multi-view review, whose P0–P3 roadmap is fully shipped.
